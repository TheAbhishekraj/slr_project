ABSTRACT:
The feedback of position and attitude information is critical for the autonomous control of a quadrotor unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV). However, in GPS-denied environments, it is difficult to obtain the quadrotor UAV's position. The pose estimation problem for a quadrotor UAV is addressed in this paper. A miniature laser range finder and a miniature attitude and heading reference system (AHRS) unit are employed as the primary onboard sensors. A novel pose estimation algorithm is developed to provide accurate estimates of the UAV's position and attitude, which can be directly utilized by the onboard flight controller in GPS-denied environments.

KEY WORDS:
Quadrotor, Laser Range Finder, Pose Estimation, Localization, GPS-Denied Navigation

1. INTRODUCTION
In recent years, quadrotor UAVs have attracted widespread attention due to their compact structure, high maneuverability, vertical takeoff and landing (VTOL), and hovering capabilities. However, in indoor, subterranean, or urban canyon environments, Global Positioning System (GPS) signals are severely degraded or completely unavailable. For fully autonomous flight in GPS-denied environments, the vehicle must rely on onboard sensors to perform simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) or pose estimation. 

While vision sensors are lightweight, they suffer from illumination sensitivity, high computational requirements, and scale ambiguity in monocular setups. Laser range finders provide accurate, direct distance measurements regardless of ambient lighting conditions. However, typical LiDAR systems are heavy and power-hungry, making them difficult to deploy on micro/small quadrotors. This paper addresses this challenge by employing a lightweight miniature laser scanner coupled with an AHRS and developing an efficient pose estimation algorithm running onboard a digital signal processor (DSP).

2. QUADROTOR UAV PLATFORM & SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
The experimental platform is a customized quadrotor UAV equipped with:
- Frame & Propulsion: Cross-configured quadrotor airframe with high-efficiency brushless DC motors and electronic speed controllers (ESCs).
- Onboard Processing: High-performance Digital Signal Processor (TI TMS320F28335 DSP) running at 150 MHz, executing sensor acquisition, scan matching, state estimation, and attitude/position control loops.
- Sensor Suite:
  1. Miniature Laser Range Finder (LRF): Hokuyo URG-04LX scanning laser range finder, operating at 10 Hz with a 240° field of view, 0.36° angular resolution, detection range from 0.02 m to 4.0 m, and mass of only 160 g.
  2. Miniature AHRS: Tri-axial gyroscopes, accelerometers, and magnetometers providing high-rate (100 Hz) attitude angles (roll, pitch, yaw).
  3. Ultrasonic Altimeter: Downward-facing sonar for altitude (z-axis) hold.

3. POSE ESTIMATION ALGORITHM
3.1 Attitude Decoupling & Coordinate Transformation
When the quadrotor maneuvers, roll and pitch tilts cause the laser beam plane to deviate from the horizontal plane. To decouple rotational dynamics from 2D horizontal localization:
- The high-rate roll (phi) and pitch (theta) angles from the AHRS are used to project raw 3D laser range measurements onto a virtual horizontal navigation plane.
- This projection eliminates distortion caused by vehicle tilt and simplifies subsequent scan matching into a 3-DOF planar registration problem (translation x, y, and yaw psi).

3.2 Polar Scan Matching (PSM) and Point Association
Instead of standard Cartesian Iterative Closest Point (ICP), which requires high computation and is prone to local minima:
- A Polar Scan Matching (PSM) formulation is adopted, directly matching range measurements along bearing angles.
- For consecutive scans at time t-1 (reference) and time t (current), range differences are evaluated using bearing interpolation and radial consistency checks to establish valid point correspondences while rejecting dynamic outliers and occlusions.

3.3 Weighted Least-Squares State Estimation
A weighted least-squares objective function is established to minimize radial projection residuals:
- The Jacobian matrix is formulated with respect to [delta_x, delta_y, delta_psi].
- Measurement weights W = diag(w1, w2, ..., wn) are assigned based on range measurement variance and incidence angle.
- The closed-form iterative solution solves for the incremental translation (xc, yc) and rotation (psi_c) until convergence.

4. EXPERIMENTAL VALIDATION & RESULTS
The algorithm was implemented on the 150 MHz DSP and verified in real-world indoor flights:
- Test Environment: Standard indoor laboratory room (approx. 6 m x 8 m) containing walls, desks, and obstacles without external positioning beacons or motion capture markers.
- Real-Time Performance: The average execution time of the pose estimation algorithm is ~36 ms per laser frame, easily meeting the 100 ms (10 Hz) update period of the Hokuyo scanner.
- Localization Accuracy:
  * Maximum X-axis position error: -12.63 cm
  * Maximum Y-axis position error: +8.84 cm
  * Maximum Heading (yaw) error: 7.09 degrees
- Comparative Performance: Compared with standard Cartesian ICP, the proposed method reduces execution time by over 45% while achieving superior convergence stability under rapid quadrotor yaw rotations.

5. CONCLUSION
This paper presented a lightweight pose estimation system and algorithm for quadrotor UAVs in GPS-denied environments utilizing a miniature laser range finder and AHRS. Real flight experiments validated that the system achieves centimeter-level horizontal localization accuracy in real time on an embedded DSP, providing an effective foundation for autonomous indoor UAV navigation.
