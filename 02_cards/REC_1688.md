## 2. Problem Statement
"The feedback of position and attitude information is critical for the autonomous control of a quadrotor unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV). However, in GPS-denied environments, it is difficult to obtain the quadrotor UAV's position." [p.1]

## 3. Motivation
"The pose estimation problem for a quadrotor UAV is addressed in this paper. A miniature laser range finder and a miniature attitude and heading reference system (AHRS) unit are employed as the primary onboard sensors." [p.1]

## 6. System Architecture
- Platform: Custom cross-configuration quadrotor UAV equipped with high-efficiency brushless DC motors.
- Sensors: Miniature Laser Range Finder (Hokuyo URG-04LX, 160 g, 240 deg FOV), miniature AHRS (100 Hz attitude angles), ultrasonic altimeter.
- Compute: Onboard Texas Instruments TMS320F28335 DSP operating at 150 MHz executing all sensing, matching, and flight control routines.
- Communication: Wireless serial link for ground telemetry monitoring.

## 7. Experimental Setup
- real_or_sim: REAL
- environment: Indoor laboratory room (approx. 6 m x 8 m) containing walls, desks, and obstacles with no external positioning infrastructure or motion capture markers.
- trials: Dynamic real-flight trajectories including sudden aggressive yaw rotations and fast translational maneuvers.
- baselines: Standard Cartesian Iterative Closest Point (ICP) and dead reckoning odometry.
- dataset: Real onboard sensor data stream logged during indoor flights.
