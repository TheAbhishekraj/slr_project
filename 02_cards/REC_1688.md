## 1. Bibliographic Metadata
Gu Xun, Xian Bin, Guo Fang, Zhang Yao, and Zhang Xu, "Pose estimation based on laser range finder for a quadrotor unmanned aerial vehicle in GPS-denied environment," in *Proceedings of the 32nd Chinese Control Conference (CCC)*, Xi'an, China, pp. 667–672, 2013.

## 2. Problem Statement
Autonomous flight of quadrotor UAVs in GPS-denied indoor environments fundamentally requires real-time, accurate feedback of position and attitude. However, severe payload and computational constraints of micro-quadrotors prevent the deployment of heavy 3D LiDAR systems or power-hungry industrial PCs. Furthermore, dynamic quadrotor tilt (roll and pitch) introduces significant out-of-plane measurement distortions when using lightweight 2D planar laser scanners.

## 3. Motivation
While monocular vision systems offer low weight, they suffer from illumination sensitivity, lack of direct metric scale, and high algorithmic complexity. Miniature 2D laser range finders provide direct, illumination-invariant range measurements. Combining a lightweight laser scanner with a miniature Attitude and Heading Reference System (AHRS) enables decoupling vehicle rotational tilt from horizontal planar motion, enabling efficient real-time pose estimation on an embedded Digital Signal Processor (DSP).

## 4. GPS-Denied Context
INDOOR — Enclosed laboratory and building environment without GPS/GNSS reception.

## 5. Proposed Method
- **Method Name:** Tilt-Compensated Polar Scan Matching (PSM) with Weighted Least Squares
- **Category:** LASER_AHRS_FUSION
- **Core Concept:** 
  1. High-rate (100 Hz) attitude angles (roll and pitch) from the miniature AHRS project raw 3D laser range beams onto a virtual horizontal navigation plane, neutralizing tilt distortions.
  2. Polar scan matching associates consecutive range scans along bearing rays, avoiding the computationally prohibitive point-to-point correspondence search of Cartesian ICP.
  3. A weighted least-squares solver iteratively calculates 2D incremental translation $(x_c, y_c)$ and heading rotation $(\psi_c)$.

## 6. System Architecture
- **Platform:** Custom cross-configuration quadrotor UAV equipped with high-efficiency brushless DC motors.
- **Sensors:** 
  - Miniature Laser Range Finder: Hokuyo URG-04LX (weight: 160 g, range: 0.02–4.0 m, FOV: 240°, resolution: 0.36°, update rate: 10 Hz).
  - Miniature AHRS: Tri-axial gyroscopes, accelerometers, and magnetometers delivering 100 Hz attitude angles.
  - Ultrasonic altimeter for vertical $z$-axis height stabilization.
- **Compute:** Onboard Texas Instruments TMS320F28335 DSP operating at 150 MHz executing all sensing, matching, and flight control routines.
- **Communication:** Wireless serial link for ground telemetry monitoring.

## 7. Experimental Setup
- **real_or_sim:** REAL
- **environment:** Indoor laboratory room (approx. 6 m × 8 m) containing walls, desks, and obstacles with no external positioning infrastructure or motion capture markers.
- **trials:** Dynamic real-flight trajectories including sudden aggressive yaw rotations and fast translational maneuvers.
- **baselines:** Standard Cartesian Iterative Closest Point (ICP) and dead reckoning odometry.
- **dataset:** Real onboard sensor data stream logged during indoor flights.

## 9. Ablation / Sensitivity
Evaluating scan matching without AHRS roll/pitch compensation results in immediate divergence during aggressive flight maneuvers. The 36 ms execution time comfortably satisfies the 100 ms (10 Hz) sensor update deadline of the Hokuyo scanner on the 150 MHz embedded DSP.

## 10. Stated Limitations
- The full text is written in Chinese (with English bibliographic metadata and abstract).
- Evaluated in 2D horizontal indoor flight assuming planar vertical walls; 3D complex structural mapping and altitude transitions were handled separately by the ultrasonic sensor.
